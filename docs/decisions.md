# Decisions

What we decided, why, and what we turned down. Newest first. Products that haven't launched go by the name of their mark.

## 2026-11-17 (later): Tern joins, keeping its t and cursor

- **Why:** Tern, my macOS terminal, had an icon from before the language: a lowercase t and an amber cursor on a blue-grey tile.
- **Mark:** the **t and cursor**, kept, in three pieces: the stem with its hooked foot, the crossbar under it a step down the ladder (0.9), and the cursor lit. The pieces are the old icon's. The cursor grew from two thirds of the strokes' weight to five sixths, and its gap to the t's foot widened by half.
- **A second mark made of a letter,** after the site's initials. I looked at marks from Tern's world first and came back to the t: it is the icon people already have in their Dock, and the cursor says "terminal" without drawing every terminal's chevron.
- **Accent:** **amber** `#F5B83D` (`#8F6207` as a light-mode tint), the colour Tern already gives its cursor and a session that is waiting on you. It sits 22° from Avia's terracotta and 32° from the comic's vermilion, and is much lighter than both. It shares its hue with the shelf's gold, which is allowed because the shelf is a lab tool, and means the same thing as the lab tools' "waiting on you" amber.
- **Known cost:** the light-mode shades are mustard brown (`#B67D09` for the cursor on its tile), the reason we turned Marigold down for Lyrate. Accepted here: amber is already Tern's colour, and nothing else from its world was free.
- **Tier:** market. Tern is public, so it keeps clear of the other market apps and the site.
- Turned down, as marks: **Prompt** (a chevron and a lit cursor; the icon of every terminal), a **tern in profile** with its bill lit and the same bird **hovering over the cursor** (illustrations, not marks, and a smudge at 20 px), **Needs you** (three sidebar rows with one lit; it read as a to-do list and its rows are the cited line's), a **winged t** (the t's crossbar drawn as a pair of wings, to say bird, letter and prompt at once; the wings went soft below 29 px and the swept version read as an aeroplane) and a **wing prompt** (the chevron as two wings; a brushed chevron to anyone not told). Dropped as sketches: the bird from above (an aeroplane) and a bird over a horizon line (mountains).
- The crossbar at 0.6, as the site's r is, was also turned down: the t stopped reading as one letter.

## 2026-11-17: the site joins, with initials and Sky

- **Why:** I redesigned my site before the language was written down. Its text was navy and cream, its wordmark Outfit, and its mark "IR" set in type on a navy tile.
- **Mark:** **Initials**, "ir" in three pieces: the i's stem, an r drawn as one hooked stroke, and the i's dot lit. It was the only mark made of letters until Tern's t joined later the same day. We turned down letters built from units for the shelf and the cited line, whose worlds had better subjects; the site is a person, so the name is its world.
- **Accent:** **Sky** `#7DBEE8` (`#1E71A7` as a light-mode tint). I chose it over ink only after seeing both on the home page. It sits 20° from the Throw's blue and 28° from Lyrate's sea glass. We accepted that: the shapes differ, as the comic's red and Avia's terracotta do at 10°. Turned down: **ink only** (as the face does it; the lit dot no longer stood apart from the stem) and **Rose** `#E39AAE` (22° from the shared critical coral).
- **Known cost:** the light tint is close to a browser's default link blue, and the light icon tile is a pale blue-grey.
- **Tier:** a third tier, `site`. The site is public and shows the market apps together, so it keeps clear of them as a market app would.
- Turned down, as marks: **North-east** (a compass needle with its north-east half lit; it read as a location arrow and echoed Safari's needle while Avia is a browser), **Trend** (a line heading up and to the right; the stock-chart glyph of every finance app) and **Field** (nine data points brightening toward the north-east; two pieces over the limit of seven, and busy beside the name). Dropped as sketches: rising bars (phone signal, and the shelf), a lotus (a spa logo) and a lone i (the info symbol).

## 2026-11-16: the projector's mark becomes the Throw

- **Why change:** the screen and three seats read as a display with three buttons (a remote, a pager), most of all on the light tile. The seats were small and floated clear of the screen, nothing overlapped, and its rectangles were the comic panels' vocabulary.
- **Mark:** the **Throw**, a projector's beam landing on a slim lit screen. The beam narrows to the lens at the right, so it is also Play. Three bands, all 13 wide and 4 apart (the gap to the screen too), run at full strength from an ink tip to the screen's blue `#84A3F4`, so the beam takes on the screen's colour as it lands.
- **A second exception to "one lit",** after Lyrate's Riffle. Two market apps now share the ink-to-accent fade; shape and colour keep them apart.
- Turned down, as marks: **Back row** (the screen seen over three seat backs; the smallest change, still a stack of rectangles), **Sofa** (the only one that says "home", but the sofa softens to a block at 20 px) and **Movie night** (two heads over a sofa back; a "photo" glyph when small). Dropped as sketches: the screen's light on the floor (a laptop), the room from the side (a bar chart), a ticket, the room in perspective (an envelope).
- Turned down, of the beam: the bands in ink stepping down the ladder (the first drawing), two bands, a solid beam (a plain transport button), three bands in full ink (the light no longer fades), the fade reversed, a lens dot at the tip (least like Play), softer corners, a tilted beam (it became Lyrate's fan), and the screen as wide as a band (it stopped reading as the screen).
- **Known neighbours:** Emby, Jellyfin and VLC each use a triangle. Accepted: none pairs it with a screen or cuts it into bands.

## 2026-11-15: the open book

- **Mark:** an open book seen from the front: two pages (ink 0.9 and 0.6) and a cover (0.32) dipping to the spine, with a bookmark ribbon lit. Turned down: two flat pages side by side (they read as two books), pages rotated apart (splayed the wrong way), and the ribbon hanging out of the spine (lost at 20 px).
- **Accent:** Lyrate's sea glass `#6CC4BE`, because both are for reading. The book is a lab tool, so sharing a market app's accent is allowed. It sits beside the cited line's teal in the lab tools; the shapes keep them apart.

## 2026-11-14 (later): Lyrate moves to sea glass

- **Accent:** **sea glass** `#6CC4BE` (`#307772` as a light-mode tint) replaces orchid, which I didn't like once it was on a phone. We compared Marigold `#E8C062` (a highlighter, but its light tint turned mustard-brown and it sat 24° from Avia), Rose `#E39AAE` (22° from the shared critical coral, so it would sit beside delete actions) and Moss `#B3BF6A` (nearly the dish's lime, with a drab olive light tint). Sea glass is the only one clear of all three warm market accents and the shared state colours. It sits 10° from the cited line's teal, which is allowed because that's a lab tool.
- **Mark:** with a darker accent on the front page, the fan lost the bright cream leading edge the original icon had. Now every page is at full strength, fading from an ink front page (cream in dark mode, navy in light) to a sea-glass back page. This is the first exception to "one lit". Turned down: the front page lit (as first shipped; it reads dim), a single-colour fan in the accent (flat, and cold beside the cream), the back page lit (an underline at small sizes), and the original navy tile (it leaves the shared Night tile).
- **Stats:** the busiest hour in Lyrate's "When you read" chart is drawn in the accent.

## 2026-11-14: the first design language

The starting point was nine products with similar instincts (dark grounds, a parchment cream, one warm accent each), typed out several slightly different ways: six blacks, five creams, two approaches to text. Lyrate, Avia, the comic panels and the dish had finished icons; the shelf had a rough mark; the cited line and the tyre had none; the projector had a first mock.

### Marks

- **Grammar:** a few flat rounded pieces, one lit, the rest on the ladder 1 · 0.9 · 0.6 · 0.32 · 0.2. We found it in the existing marks rather than inventing it: the comic panels and Lyrate's pages already stepped down this way.
- **An accent per product, from its world** ("domain accents"). Turned down: one shared accent for every mark (the strongest family signal, but it collided with the critical colour and took the lab tools' colours away), and ink-only marks (the calmest, but the comic lost its red and small icons were told apart by shape alone).
- **Two tiers.** Market apps must be distinct from each other; lab tools may overlap. The comic's vermilion and Avia's terracotta sit 10° apart and stay: they differ in lightness and shape.
- **The dish:** kept, moved from amber to **lime** `#BBD07C`. Amber is the lab tools' "waiting on you" colour and sat beside the shelf's gold. Lime had the most room on the wheel. Its light shades are olive (`#607329` as a tint), which we accepted. Turned down: a hub of orbiting dots and a lit-window house; cyan (it crowded the cited line and the projector); orchid (then Lyrate's).
- **Lyrate:** **orchid** `#CF9BE0` on its front page. It had no accent; ink only stopped working once text followed the system, where a cream tint can't show a selection next to white labels. Pink highlighter came first; orchid was chosen. Its tile moved from navy to Night. (Replaced by sea glass the same day, above.)
- **The projector:** a screen and one row of three seats, as first drawn; screen blue `#84A3F4`. (Replaced by the Throw on 2026-11-16, above.)
- **The shelf:** three spines stepping down, a fourth leaning in gold. Turned down: an initial with a leaning book; a catalogue card (generic).
- **The cited line:** a page of lines, one passage lit in teal `#5FBFA8`, for "every claim cites a passage". Teal replaced sage, which is the shared "fine" state. Turned down: an initial built from units; a staircase of blocks.
- **The tyre:** in real proportion (the wheel about 0.57 of the tyre), no spokes, dark rubber in dark mode, the violet compound band in two arcs. Turned down: speed lines (they read as a stack), a chequer, and two drawings of a race strategy (a pit-lane dive that read as a "U", and an under-pass that was too abstract); spokes (lost below 40 px).
- **Avia:** keeps its tent art; only the light-mode tile changes.

### Colour

- **P2: system text, our grounds, cream as a light.** Text and icons use the platform's label colours, on the web too, mirroring Apple's values. The grounds stay ours, sheets included; light mode stays Light paper `#F2EDED`. Cream becomes the fill of lit things and the primary button. Each app's accent is its tint.
  - Why: screenshots showed iOS's own pieces (Lyrate's tab bar and search button, Avia's Settings header) in white beside the app's cream text. The code agreed: Lyrate and Avia used system text colours in about 340 places and cream in about 126, while one lab tool used cream almost everywhere and system colours never.
  - Turned down: **P1, fully native** (no clash at all, but the warmth goes and the apps look like any iOS app), and **P3, cream everywhere** (the warmest, but every system piece needs theming one by one, and some can't be).
- **Pastel accents** kept: they're the more tasteful choice.

### Icons in light mode

- **A cream tile washed 14% with the accent, Navy pieces, and the lit piece in the accent's 3:1 shade.** Dark mode keeps the Night tile.
- Turned down: the Night tile in both appearances (one icon, but no identity on a light home screen), an accent-coloured tile with dark pieces (the "one lit" idea disappears and the mark flips between modes), plain cream (kept as the quieter fallback) and a 26% wash (it drifts toward the accent tile).

### Type

- **Geist Medium** for names and the lab tools' display numbers, in sentence case. Market apps follow it for now but may change it.
- Body text is each product's choice.
- We compared nine faces; the finalists were Sora, Geist and Figtree. Outfit, the face until then, wasn't shortlisted.
